# BMB Closed-Universe Forensic Audit Protocol (Report-Against-Itself Method)

This protocol supplements the 33-point violation analysis. It governs the FACTUAL layer of every
dispute package: the one-round factual dispute letter, the plain-language dispute wording on each
violation, and the Markup Map.

## Core method

- This is a ONE-ROUND dispute. Do not create round 1 / round 2 strategies or suggest follow-up rounds.
- Use ONLY information that appears on the credit report itself. No bank statements, account
  statements, outside documents, creditor websites, personal assumptions, or guessed corrections.
- The audit universe is limited to: the Account Information section, the Payment History grid,
  Remarks / Comments / Status / Removal date / Date updated and other report fields, and — when
  multiple bureau reports are provided — the same account across bureaus.
- We are NOT trying to guess the "true" answer. We are testing whether the bureau's own reporting is
  missing, blank, incomplete, inaccurate, contradictory, internally inconsistent, confusing, or
  unsupported by its own report presentation. Stay inside the four corners of the report.

## Audit philosophy

Treat the credit report as a self-contained document:
1. Audit each tradeline field by field.
2. Analyze the payment history grid by itself (missing months, blanks, gaps, illogical delinquency
   progression, unexplained resets, repeated charge-off reporting, updates after closure).
3. Compare the payment history grid to the Account Information section (last payment date/amount vs
   grid, status vs progression, charge-off vs remarks, balance vs past due, closed status vs
   continued updates, DOFD vs visible delinquency sequence, removal date vs delinquency timing,
   "paid in full" vs charge-off/past due).
4. Compare status, remarks, dates, balances, and delinquency progression for logical consistency.
5. If multiple bureau reports are provided, compare the same account across bureaus for material
   inconsistencies (balances, dates, statuses, histories, remarks, limits, DOFD, closure/removal
   dates, and data present on one report but missing on another).
6. Turn each issue into a concise numbered factual dispute point.

## Issue classification

Classify every issue as exactly one of: Missing field · Blank field · Incomplete field ·
Internal inconsistency · Contradiction between account info and payment history ·
Contradiction within payment history · Cross-bureau inconsistency ·
Potential reporting issue requiring clarification.

Do not automatically call every blank field illegal or false. State precisely WHY the field is
challenged: it is missing, it conflicts with another part of the same report, it makes the reporting
incomplete or contradictory, or the tradeline cannot be understood or verified from the face of the
report.

## Writing style for the factual letter

Direct, plain English, field-specific. No fluff, no long legal essay, no aggressive threats, no
made-up legal conclusions, no overexplaining. Each numbered dispute item corresponds to ONE specific
questioned field or contradiction, phrased as a short factual question or statement:

- "What was the date of first delinquency? Please fix or delete this entire account."
- "What was the monthly payment? Please fix or delete this entire account."
- "The last payment date conflicts with the payment history grid. How can both be true?
  Please fix or delete this entire account."
- "The account is listed in charge-off status, but the remarks say 'paid in full.' This is
  contradictory. Please fix or delete this entire account."

The letter body: identifies the account, states that the consumer reviewed the report and found
inaccurate / missing / incomplete / contradictory information, requests investigation and
correction, requests deletion if the information cannot be verified or corrected, requests an
updated report, and requests notification to recipients of the report in the past six months.

## Markup rules

- Every numbered dispute item MUST map to at least one visual markup location: the PDF page number,
  the report section, and the exact text/field/month cell to draw a red box around.
- The callout number written next to each red box is the SAME number as the dispute item.
- If a contradiction involves two locations (a field in Account Information and a cell in the
  Payment History grid), both locations are boxed and both get the same callout number.

## Hard rules

- Do not invent missing dates, balances, or payment amounts — phrase missing data as a question.
- Do not assume a value is wrong just because it is unfamiliar.
- Do not say "fraud" or "identity theft" unless the report itself supports it.
- Do not write a multi-round strategy or a legal memo in the factual letter.
- Match every numbered dispute item to a visual markup item.
